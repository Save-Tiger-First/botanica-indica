## 1. Executive recommendation

STF should use the next 12 months to establish organizational credibility, complete the scientific foundations of Botanica Indica, validate a narrow first product, and prepare—not prematurely scale—one carefully selected conservation or learning pilot.

The plan deliberately avoids launching multiple apps, running several unrelated programmes, or committing to a large technology build before the scientific model, rights framework, user need and delivery capacity are ready.

### Recommended order

1. **Governance and organizational readiness** — establish who is accountable, how decisions are made, and what STF can responsibly undertake.
2. **Botanica Indica scientific and product readiness** — reconcile the scientific data model, secure independent review, define MVP boundaries and validate real Indian use cases.
3. **Partnership and pilot readiness** — identify credible partners, select one pilot only if it passes the evidence, rights, feasibility and evaluation gates.
4. **Public communication and learning** — communicate science accurately and accessibly, including possible art–science interpretation as a small optional partnership experiment.
5. **Evaluation and next-year decision** — publish a transparent report and decide what to continue, change, pause or stop.

## 2. Assumptions and planning constraints

This is a planning model, not a statement of STF's current legal, financial or staffing position. The following assumptions must be verified by the governing body before adoption:

- STF has or will establish a lawful organizational structure capable of entering contracts, receiving and spending funds, employing or engaging people, and accepting programme responsibilities.
- No external funding, institutional partnership, scientific endorsement or community consent is assumed.
- Botanica Indica remains the first domain priority.
- Existing documentation and repository work are planning assets, not proof that the software or scientific model is production-ready.
- No Task 05E physical database design or production deployment should be authorized until the outstanding scientific data-model issues, expert review and readiness gates are closed.
- The plan assumes a small, distributed team with a mix of paid coordination and specialist contracts; volunteer availability is not treated as guaranteed capacity.
- The art–science concept “Hidden Connections” is optional and should proceed only if it does not displace core scientific, governance or product work.
- Budget ranges are indicative Indian rupee planning estimates, not vendor quotations, salary benchmarks or financial advice. Obtain local quotes and tax/accounting advice.

## 3. Twelve-month outcomes

By the end of Month 12, STF should aim to have:

1. A documented governance and accountability structure, with decision rights and conflict-of-interest rules.
2. A reviewed organizational risk, legal, finance, safeguarding and data-protection register.
3. An independent scientific review and disposition record for the Botanica Indica logical model and blocking review findings.
4. A defined MVP scope, product success criteria and user research evidence.
5. A rights-aware source, dataset and media registry design with operational procedures.
6. A tested narrow prototype or MVP only if the scientific and technical gates are satisfied.
7. A qualified partner shortlist and, at most, one pilot concept approved for implementation.
8. Baseline and evaluation methods established before any pilot intervention.
9. A public-facing explanation of STF's purpose and evidence standards that distinguishes aspirations from active work and measured outcomes.
10. A transparent annual report and a resourced Year 2 recommendation.

These are targets for planning. They are not promises that ecological outcomes will be achieved within one year.

## 4. Workstreams, accountable roles and decision rights

Roles below are proposed functions. One person may initially cover more than one function, but scientific approval, financial authorization and independent evaluation should not be conflated.

| Workstream | Accountable role | Supporting roles | Key output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governance and strategy | Governing body / designated executive lead | Legal and governance adviser | Approved charter, decision register, risk oversight |
| Programme delivery | Programme lead | Project coordinator, workstream owners | Integrated plan, milestones, issue log |
| Botanica Indica science | Scientific lead / taxonomy adviser | Botanists, biodiversity informatics specialists, data architect | Scientific model review and acceptance record |
| Product and technology | Product/technology lead | UX researcher, software engineer, security adviser | MVP scope, prototype, test and release evidence |
| Data rights and legal | Data governance lead with qualified counsel | Privacy adviser, source-rights specialist | Rights register, legal applicability notes, agreements |
| Partnerships and community engagement | Partnerships/community lead | Local facilitators, community-selected representatives | Partner due diligence, consent and engagement plans |
| Finance and operations | Finance/operations lead | Accountant, HR/administration support | Budget, controls, contracts, reporting |
| Monitoring, evaluation and learning | MEL lead / independent evaluator | Scientific lead, pilot partner | Baselines, indicator protocols, evaluation report |
| Communications and creative practice | Communications lead | Scientists, educators, paid artists | Evidence-reviewed public communication |

**Independence safeguard:** The person responsible for delivering a pilot should not be the sole person validating its claimed impact. Use an independent reviewer where the significance or sensitivity of the claim warrants it.

## 5. Month-by-month roadmap

### Quarter 1 — Establish the foundations (Months 1–3)

| Month | Main actions | Deliverables / evidence | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm legal and organizational status; appoint accountable executive and workstream owners; review mission and strategic framework; establish decision, conflict-of-interest and risk registers; confirm available funding and capacity | Governance pack; role descriptions; decision log; initial risk register; verified organizational facts | Gate 0: authority, capacity and legal status understood |
| 2 | Commission independent review of Task 05 scientific data model and DR-01–DR-12 findings; identify appropriate botanical taxonomy, biodiversity informatics and data governance reviewers; begin legal review of data and biological-resource activities | Reviewer terms of reference; review schedule; issue-by-issue disposition matrix; legal questions register | Gate 1: review scope and reviewers accepted |
| 3 | Hold structured scientific review; conduct user and stakeholder discovery; map rights and source constraints; draft MVP hypotheses and product success measures | Scientific review report or documented review in progress; user research synthesis; initial rights registry schema; MVP problem statement | Gate 2: no unresolved critical issue hidden or waived |

**Quarter 1 no-go conditions:** Do not proceed to physical database design, public scientific claims, large-scale data ingestion or production AI training if blocking scientific or rights issues remain unresolved.

### Quarter 2 — Reconcile and validate the first domain (Months 4–6)

| Month | Main actions | Deliverables / evidence | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Resolve blocking model issues with documented rationale; revise Task 05B/05C/05D as approved; map internal data to external standards only where semantically valid; define source and media rights handling | Reconciliation decisions; updated logical model; test cases; rights and provenance rules | Gate 3: scientific and data model approval for next stage |
| 5 | Define narrow MVP scope; select representative Indian taxa and geographies; design identification and abstention flows; prototype user journeys; threat-model sensitive data | MVP specification; test dataset rights record; UX prototype; security/privacy threat model | Gate 4: product scope, data rights and safety approved |
| 6 | Build or configure a limited prototype; test identification and information quality; conduct usability sessions; assess performance by geography, taxonomic group, image quality and user type | Prototype; evaluation dataset documentation; test report; issue backlog; release recommendation | Gate 5: prototype evidence supports or rejects MVP investment |

**Quarter 2 no-go conditions:** No claim of “expert validated” without named, qualified reviewers and documented scope. No AI accuracy headline without a representative evaluation design, uncertainty reporting and abstention results.

### Quarter 3 — Controlled validation and partnership readiness (Months 7–9)

| Month | Main actions | Deliverables / evidence | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Improve prototype based on evidence; complete source, taxonomy update and correction workflows; prepare user support and moderation procedures | Updated prototype; correction workflow; operational runbook; source update schedule | Gate 6: operational readiness review |
| 8 | Conduct controlled beta with a defined user cohort; assess accessibility, reliability, scientific quality and privacy; complete partner due diligence for potential pilot themes | Beta evaluation; accessibility findings; partner due diligence; pilot options paper | Gate 7: beta and partner suitability |
| 9 | Score pilot options; consult relevant local communities and institutions through appropriate processes; choose no more than one pilot if it passes all no-go tests; define baseline and monitoring protocol | Pilot selection record; signed partner terms if approved; permissions; baseline protocol; risk and maintenance plan | Gate 8: pilot authorization or explicit decision not to proceed |

Pilot themes may include urban biodiversity, local plant learning, or an Art × Ecology × Science communication experiment. The theme should be selected on local evidence and partner readiness—not because it is currently fashionable or appears in a talk.

### Quarter 4 — Execute selectively, evaluate and decide (Months 10–12)

| Month | Main actions | Deliverables / evidence | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | If authorized, begin one limited pilot; otherwise focus on Botanica Indica validation and partnerships; record implementation fidelity, participation and issues | Pilot start report or documented no-go; baseline record; incident log; expenditure tracking |
| 11 | Complete pilot learning review; assess user and partner outcomes; conduct scientific and rights audit; prepare Year 2 options and funding requirements | Interim evaluation; audit findings; partner feedback; Year 2 options paper |
| 12 | Publish annual report; classify results as activities, outputs, outcomes or ecological evidence; governing body reviews portfolio; approve, redesign, pause or stop workstreams | Annual report; financial summary; impact limitations; Year 2 plan and budget; formal decisions | Gate 9: continuation and scale decision |

If a pilot requires a longer ecological observation period, report the baseline and early implementation honestly. Do not force an ecological impact conclusion into a 12-month reporting cycle.

## 6. Critical dependencies and sequencing

The following dependencies are non-negotiable:

1. Organizational authority and legal capacity precede contracts, fundraising commitments and field activities.
2. Scientific model reconciliation and independent review precede physical schema freeze and production data migration.
3. Source and media rights assessment precedes ingestion, redistribution, publication or AI training.
4. Sensitive-location threat assessment precedes public maps, APIs or downloadable datasets.
5. Representative test data and documented evaluation precede identification performance claims.
6. Community engagement and appropriate consent precede collection or use of community-held knowledge.
7. Partner roles, permissions, baseline and maintenance responsibilities precede a conservation intervention.
8. Evaluation design precedes pilot implementation—not the other way around.
9. Independent review precedes strong public claims about scientific validation or ecological impact.

## 7. Indicative budget framework (INR)

The following figures are deliberately broad planning envelopes for a small India-based initiative. They are not market quotations. Actual costs depend on legal form, location, team seniority, employment/contracting model, travel, taxes, technology choices and partner contributions.

### 7.1 Budget scenarios

| Scenario | Indicative 12-month envelope | What it could support | Main limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Lean validation | ₹18–30 lakh | Part-time programme coordination, essential scientific/legal reviews, limited user research, narrow prototype, basic operations | Little capacity for field pilots or sustained engineering |
| Core recommended | ₹35–60 lakh | Part-time/selected full-time delivery capacity, independent scientific review, MVP prototype, rights/legal work, user testing and one small pilot only if ready | Requires disciplined scope and staged release of funds |
| Expanded | ₹70 lakh–₹1.10 crore | Larger specialist team, stronger engineering and evaluation capacity, broader field partnerships and potentially one substantial pilot | Not recommended until demand, governance and funding are proven |

**Recommendation:** Plan against the core scenario as a ceiling for fundraising discussions, but authorize expenditure in stages. If STF does not yet have confirmed funding or organizational capacity, use the lean validation scenario and defer field intervention.

### 7.2 Indicative core-scenario cost categories

| Cost category | Indicative range |
|---|---:|
| Programme coordination and operations | ₹6–9 lakh |
| Scientific review and specialist honoraria | ₹4–7 lakh |
| Product, UX and software engineering | ₹9–15 lakh |
| Data rights, legal, privacy and governance advice | ₹3–5 lakh |
| User research, accessibility and testing | ₹2–3 lakh |
| Partnership development and limited field engagement | ₹3–5 lakh |
| Monitoring, evaluation and independent review | ₹2–4 lakh |
| Communications, learning materials and documentation | ₹1.5–3 lakh |
| Hosting, software, security and administration | ₹1–2 lakh |
| Contingency | ₹3.5–7 lakh |
| **Indicative total** | **₹35–60 lakh** |

The category ranges are approximate and should be re-costed. They are not intended to be summed at their independent maximums. Exclude major hardware purchases, large-scale model training, permanent exhibition infrastructure, long-term ecological monitoring and multi-city programmes unless separately costed and approved.

### 7.3 Budget control rules

- Approve an annual ceiling and quarterly release plan.
- Require written scopes, deliverables and acceptance criteria for consultants and vendors.
- Separate restricted grants from unrestricted funds.
- Record in-kind support separately; do not count it as cash available.
- Use dual authorization for payments above a threshold set by the governing body.
- Require conflict-of-interest declarations and documented procurement comparisons.
- Track committed, paid, forecast and remaining balances monthly.
- Do not incur commitments against anticipated grants before receipt or formal authorization.
- Maintain a contingency reserve and obtain approval before reallocating material budget lines.

## 9. Optional Art × Science × Ecology workstream

The Rebecca Rutstein case suggests a useful creative interpretation method, but it should not become a seventh major operational workstream during the initial build unless core foundations are on track.

### Proposed small experiment: Hidden Connections

Proceed only if:
- A qualified scientific partner and paid artist voluntarily agree to collaborate.
- One specific ecological theme and audience are selected.
- The source data and image rights are cleared.
- Community knowledge and cultural rights are respected.
- The budget is separately approved and does not displace Botanica Indica scientific validation.
- The project defines whether it is testing audience reach, curiosity, learning or dialogue.
- Environmental footprint, accessibility and artist independence are addressed.

**Indicative optional allocation:** ₹1.5–4 lakh for a modest, non-permanent pilot, subject to actual quotes and scope. This should be treated as part of—not additional to—the approved annual ceiling. No permanent installation is recommended in Year 1.

## 10. Year 1 decision gates and stop/go rules

| Gate | Required evidence | Decision if not met |
|---|---|---|
| G0 Organizational authority | Legal capacity, accountable leadership, budget controls | Pause external commitments |
| G1 Scientific review | Qualified reviewers and agreed scope | Do not claim validation |
| G2 Data model | Blocking findings resolved or explicitly dispositioned | Do not freeze physical design |
| G3 Rights and privacy | Source/media rights, privacy and sensitive data controls | Do not ingest or publish affected data |
| G4 MVP scope | User need, product acceptance criteria and test plan | Re-scope or stop build |
| G5 Prototype evidence | Performance, abstention, usability and security findings | Iterate; do not launch publicly |
| G6 Pilot readiness | Partner, permissions, baseline, safety, maintenance, budget | Do not implement pilot |
| G7 Scale decision | Evaluation, costs, risks and partner feedback | Continue, redesign, pause or stop |

A gate may be conditionally passed only with a named owner, deadline and explicit statement of residual risk. Legal, consent and serious ecological safety requirements cannot be waived for schedule convenience.

## 11. Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Too many initiatives | One core domain and at most one pilot |
| Volunteer dependency | Name paid accountable owners for essential functions |
| Scientific errors | Independent review, provenance, correction and abstention |
| Technology overbuild | Modular monolith, narrow MVP, stage-gated investment |
| Data rights violations | Asset-level rights registry and pre-ingestion checks |
| Sensitive species exposure | Generalization, restricted access and threat assessment |
| Community knowledge extraction | Community authority, consent, benefit-sharing and grievance process |
| Unfunded commitments | Cash-flow gate before hiring, contracting or pilot launch |
| Inflated impact claims | Contribution-based theory of change and independent evaluation |
| Artist/scientist imbalance | Paid work, creative independence and co-designed scope |
| Pilot maintenance failure | Named post-pilot owner and lifecycle funding |
| Reputational risk | Public correction policy and transparent limitations |
