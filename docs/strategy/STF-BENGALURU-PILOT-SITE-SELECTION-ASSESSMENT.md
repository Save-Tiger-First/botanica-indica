## 1. Executive conclusion

This assessment compares three candidate sites:

1. **Kaikondrahalli (also officially rendered Kaigondanahalli) Lake**, Bellandur / Mahadevapura area.
2. **Jakkur Lake**, Yelahanka area.
3. **Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake Birds Conservation Reserve**, Yelahanka.

The research supports the following preliminary ranking for a first STF pilot that is limited to non-invasive, permission-based biodiversity observations and public learning:

| Preliminary priority | Candidate | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — first due-diligence candidate | Kaikondrahalli / Kaigondanahalli Lake | Best potential balance of a public lake setting, existing civic/ecological stewardship, and an opportunity to test a clearly complementary plant–bird–butterfly baseline. Must first establish whether an additional survey would fill a genuine gap rather than duplicate existing work. |
| 2 — partnership-led alternative | Jakkur Lake | Strong citizen engagement, an active community organisation, and an established history of nature education and citizen science. Existing activities appear extensive; STF should approach only if JaLaPoshan identifies a specific unmet need. |
| 3 — specialist-only option | Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake Birds Conservation Reserve | High bird-conservation value and a formal protected-area status, but an existing bird-survey record and clear Forest Department authority mean this is not an ordinary open-access pilot site. Consider only through an explicitly invited, Forest Department-led or approved collaboration. |

**Recommendation:** Make Kaikondrahalli the first candidate for a *desk-based gap and partner discussion*, not a final selection. Keep Jakkur as the comparator and potential alternative. Do not approach Yelahanka Puttenahalli as a routine citizen-science site; any work there should be shaped with the Karnataka Forest Department and the existing conservation trust.

No field visit for research, formal survey, volunteer recruitment, photography of people, data collection, intervention, or public announcement is authorized by this assessment. Publicly accessible paths do not automatically confer permission to conduct organized research or publish biodiversity records.

## 2. Scope and method of this assessment

This is a desk-based comparison of public sources available on 16 November 2026. It is not a site inspection, ecological survey, legal opinion, land-record verification, or confirmation from any site custodian or community group.

Evidence was considered in five dimensions:

1. Ecological and learning value.
2. Existing scientific information and the likelihood of duplication.
3. Existing community stewardship and partnership readiness.
4. Authority, legal status and permissions.
5. Practical suitability for a small, non-invasive, repeatable pilot.

The assessment distinguishes:
- **Verified from a source:** a fact explicitly stated in an identified public document or database.
- **Historical evidence:** useful context that must not be treated as current operational status.
- **Inference:** a reasoned view based on available evidence.
- **Unverified:** a fact requiring direct confirmation from the competent authority or organisation.

No numerical weighted score is assigned. The existing pilot proposal's scorecard requires site inspection, current permission evidence, safety/accessibility checks and direct stakeholder confirmation. Assigning precise scores from desk research alone would create false precision.

## 3. Candidate A — Kaikondrahalli / Kaigondanahalli Lake

### 3.1 Verified public-source findings

- BBMP's Lakes Department list identifies **Kaigondanahalli Lake** in Mahadevapura Zone and lists survey-number/area information. The same BBMP page describes 167 lakes as being in BBMP custody in the page's published administrative snapshot. The page is a BBMP source but is not evidence that the current post-GBA asset custodian or operational authority has been re-confirmed for this pilot.
- BBMP's public park portal lists **Kaikondrahalli Lake park** in Bellandur ward and gives a park-location coordinate of 12.915848, 77.673597. The entry says bird inspections were not done in that park record. This is a park-management record, not a comprehensive lake biodiversity assessment and not proof that no biodiversity work exists.
- A BBMP lake inspection entry for Kaigondanahalli Lake records an inspection dated 27 June 2024, including maintenance observations. It is historical operational information, not a current condition report.
- The National Mission for Clean Ganga's urban wetland/waterbody management guidance includes Kaikondrahalli as a case study. It describes the 2009–2012 restoration period and identifies BBMP, MAPSAS, United Way and the Revenue Department as key stakeholders in that historical programme.
- A peer-reviewed study of urban lake co-management describes BBMP, United Way Bengaluru and a local community association as actors in the lake's co-management arrangements in the period studied.
- A peer-reviewed study on Bengaluru lake communities includes Kaikondrahalli and Kasavanahalli and documents the role of local communities in restoration and stewardship.

### 3.2 Ecological and pilot suitability

**Potential strengths**
- A restored urban lake and adjacent public green space provide an appropriate setting for a carefully delimited urban biodiversity learning exercise.
- The pilot's plant, bird and butterfly groups are compatible with the type of urban biodiversity indicators in the Bengaluru Urban Forest Manual.
- A park portal record that lacks bird inspection data may indicate a possible gap in one administrative dataset, but it does not prove the absence of independent surveys or community records.
- The site offers a useful context for examining how ecological objectives, recreation, lake management and community stewardship coexist.

**Risks and limitations**
- This is an established restoration and stewardship site. STF must not claim to be introducing biodiversity monitoring or community lake stewardship to a place where these already exist.
- Existing partners may already conduct observations, events, maintenance monitoring or biodiversity work. Current activities and any existing data must be mapped before designing a survey.
- The BBMP portal's park record appears to describe a park asset and not necessarily the entire lake. Its listed area must not be represented as the lake's total area.
- Current land custody, delegated maintenance, park access conditions, any current management MoU and the authority to authorize organized research must be confirmed after the Greater Bengaluru governance transition.

### 3.3 Preliminary judgement

**Best first due-diligence candidate**, provided MAPSAS and the competent public authority identify a specific gap that the proposed pilot can responsibly fill. The most appropriate STF role may be to contribute a small, independently reviewed baseline or improve documentation/verification—not to create a new lake group or duplicate existing monitoring.

### 3.4 Required confirmations

1. Current legal land custodian and competent authority.
2. Current City Corporation / GBA responsibility for the lake and park.
3. Current MAPSAS role, scope and willingness to discuss a complementary project.
4. Whether United Way or other organisations remain involved in any current management arrangement.
5. Existing plant, bird, butterfly and other biodiversity survey datasets, with data rights.
6. Whether any current survey protocol or monitoring plan already covers the proposed indicators.
7. Permitted survey areas, times, group sizes, photography and public communication.
8. Any site-specific safety, access, event or volunteer rules.

## 4. Candidate B — Jakkur Lake

### 4.1 Verified public-source findings

- BBMP's Lakes Department Jakkur page describes prior interventions including desilting, dewatering, bunds and pathways, a constructed wetland system, treated wastewater inflow, a sedimentation trap, tree planting, bird watching, tree tagging and separation of community and conservation zones.
- The same BBMP page describes Jakkur as part of a lake series, with inflows from multiple upstream lakes and an outlet feeding downstream lakes. This reinforces that the lake must be understood as part of a hydrological network, not as an isolated park.
- JaLaPoshan's own website, accessed in November 2026, invites participation in weekly lake-side activities and events, describes bird documentation, and provides a contact channel for organisations and schools interested in participation or contribution.
- The Bengaluru Sustainability Forum's 2020–2022 progress report describes Nature's Gurukul at Jakkur as a citizen-science and expert-guided outdoor learning programme. It records tree census work, birding sessions, water testing, workshops and volunteer activities during that project period. This is historical evidence of prior activities, not confirmation of the programme's current frequency or scope.
- A peer-reviewed study on urban lake stewardship describes the Jakkur co-management experience and the lake group's choices around light, boating, birds and public events. This is historical/academic evidence and should not be treated as a current management agreement.

### 4.2 Ecological and pilot suitability

**Potential strengths**
- A well-established citizen-engagement network may make a supervised learning programme easier to discuss.
- The existing bird, tree and water-related activities create opportunities to learn from experienced practitioners.
- The connected lake-series context may offer a future systems-level learning question, if a suitable scientific partner and data are available.

**Risks and limitations**
- There is a substantial risk of duplication: prior work already includes tree census, birding, water testing and nature education.
- A new STF programme may burden volunteers or compete for attention unless it provides a clearly defined and requested contribution.
- BBMP's published interventions show that water treatment and constructed wetlands are part of the lake's management context. STF must not infer present water quality or ecological condition from the existence of these interventions.
- The current site custodian, delegated responsibilities, management arrangements, access rules and any ongoing monitoring should be verified directly.

### 4.3 Preliminary judgement

**Strong partnership-led alternative, but not the default first choice.** Contact JaLaPoshan only to ask whether there is a specific, current, unmet need—for example, a structured plant-record verification workflow, data curation, or a defined indicator not already monitored. If JaLaPoshan says existing work already covers the proposed scope, STF should not proceed at Jakkur.

### 4.4 Required confirmations

1. Current JaLaPoshan activities and whether weekly events are still being held.
2. Whether the organisation is willing to explore a defined collaboration.
3. Existing survey protocols, raw datasets, data rights and gaps.
4. Current lake asset custodian and the competent City Corporation / GBA authority.
5. Any active management MoU, work order or other delegated arrangement.
6. Whether an STF contribution would add value without duplicating existing programmes.
7. Permissions for organized observations, volunteers, photography and public dissemination.

## 5. Candidate C — Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake Birds Conservation Reserve

**Important distinction:** This is the Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake / Birds Conservation Reserve near Puttenahalli and Attur villages in Yelahanka Hobli. It is not the separate Puttenahalli Lake in JP Nagar 7th Phase, South Bengaluru.

### 5.1 Verified public-source findings

- A 2020–2021 bird survey report submitted to Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake and Bird Conservation Trust states that the Government of Karnataka notified the site as a Bird Conservation Reserve on 29 April 2015.
- The report identifies the Karnataka Forest Department as custodian and the Yelahanka Puttenahalli Lake and Bird Conservation Trust (YPLBCT) as the organisation managing the lake habitat in collaboration with the department at the time of the report.
- The survey used fortnightly visits from March 2020 to April 2021, with a defined observation method, and recorded 123 bird species. The survey's methods and results provide a useful existing baseline reference; they do not establish the site's current bird list or population status.
- A Karnataka High Court decision reported in December 2021 directed the Forest Department to look after the Birds Conservation Reserve and identified the Chief Wildlife Warden as the competent authority for control, management and maintenance.
- A report filed by the Karnataka Forest/Ecology/Environment Department before the National Green Tribunal in July 2024 states that the reserve covers approximately 37 acres, is managed by the Karnataka Forest Department, and supports over 150 bird species. It also documents a dispute about sewage inflow and proposed pipeline work, including the need for Forest Department permission and an NGT direction that no further work be carried out until necessary approvals were granted.
- The 2024 NGT filing is a party's report in litigation, not a final adjudication of every factual issue. It is strong evidence of the authority and permission sensitivities, but current status of the case and any subsequent orders must be checked before any activity.

### 5.2 Ecological and pilot suitability

**Potential strengths**
- High relevance to wetland birds and bird habitat.
- A defined protected-area context and an existing survey report provide a strong scientific starting point.
- There may be opportunities for a specialist-led contribution if the Forest Department and YPLBCT identify a genuine research or documentation gap.

**Risks and limitations**
- This is a notified Bird Conservation Reserve, not an ordinary public park.
- The competent authority and any applicable conservation-reserve management committee arrangements must be respected.
- Existing bird monitoring and a detailed 2020–2021 report make a generic bird survey duplicative unless a clear update question is identified.
- Field activity, group visits, photography, access beyond permitted public areas, disturbance-sensitive observations and data publication require explicit confirmation from the Forest Department.
- Exact nest, roost and sensitive species locations must not be publicly disclosed.
- The 2024 litigation/administrative record demonstrates that even infrastructure work around the reserve has required careful authorization. STF should not assume that a non-invasive survey is automatically exempt from permission.

### 5.3 Preliminary judgement

**Do not use as STF's first independent pilot site.** Retain it as a possible future specialist collaboration, initiated only if the Karnataka Forest Department and YPLBCT invite or approve a defined contribution. The existing bird survey makes this a particularly useful case for learning how to build on prior scientific work rather than repeat it.

### 5.4 Required confirmations

1. Current protected-area notification, boundaries and management plan.
2. Current status of the NGT proceedings and any later orders relevant to access or activities.
3. Written permission from the competent Forest Department authority / Chief Wildlife Warden as required.
4. Current YPLBCT role and any existing monitoring programme.
5. Whether the proposed activity is compatible with the conservation-reserve management plan.
6. Any limits on photography, public access, group size, timing and data publication.
7. Sensitive-species and nest/roost data handling requirements.

## 7. Recommended next actions

### Stage A — Internal preparation

Before contacting any organisation:
1. Confirm STF's legal identity, authorized representative and ability to enter a written collaboration.
2. Confirm the accountable pilot owner and whether the proposed budget ceiling is genuinely available.
3. Prepare a one-page neutral concept note that explicitly says STF is exploring a possible complementary baseline, not proposing restoration works.
4. Prepare a data-rights and non-duplication checklist.
5. Identify an independent urban ecologist/botanist who can advise before a site is selected.

### Stage B — Non-binding partner enquiries

Recommended order:
1. **MAPSAS** — ask whether Kaikondrahalli has a current biodiversity-monitoring plan and whether there is a specific gap that a short, independently reviewed baseline could fill.
2. **JaLaPoshan** — ask whether Jakkur's existing birding, tree, water and citizen-science activities leave a defined gap where STF can contribute.
3. **YPLBCT / Karnataka Forest Department** — only a carefully worded exploratory enquiry about whether any specialist-reviewed contribution is welcome; do not request a public volunteer survey or assume access.

Do not promise funding, announce partnerships, recruit participants or collect data during these enquiries.

### Stage C — Authority and legal verification

For the leading candidate, obtain:
- written confirmation of the present land/asset custodian;
- current GBA / City Corporation jurisdiction and the officer authorized to grant permission;
- current delegated management arrangement, if any;
- written permission for the precise survey activities and boundary;
- confirmation of any wildlife, wetland, biodiversity, photography, research and data requirements;
- current local Biodiversity Management Committee status and relevant Karnataka Biodiversity Board guidance, where applicable.

### Stage D — Final site decision

Only after Stage B and Stage C:
- revisit the site with the scientific lead and site-authorized representative;
- assess access, safety, habitat units, disturbance and repeatability;
- check whether existing data already answer the proposed research questions;
- complete the weighted scorecard in the main pilot proposal;
- recommend one site or explicitly recommend no-go.

**No site should be chosen if the proposed work duplicates an existing programme or cannot obtain the necessary permissions.**

## 8. Questions for partner conversations

These are neutral due-diligence questions, not assumptions about any organisation.

1. What is your current role in the lake's management or stewardship?
2. Which organisations and government bodies currently hold formal responsibility?
3. Are plant, bird, butterfly or other biodiversity surveys already being conducted?
4. Are protocols, data, reports or monitoring schedules available for review?
5. What gaps do you believe remain, and how were those gaps identified?
6. Would a short external baseline be useful, or would it duplicate existing work?
7. What permissions and safeguards would be required?
8. What data may be shared, under what rights, attribution and location-sensitivity rules?
9. Who should review any scientific methods and findings?
10. What would make a collaboration genuinely useful to the site and its users?

## 9. My recommendation and rationale

**Proceed with Kaikondrahalli as the first due-diligence conversation, while keeping Jakkur as the comparator.**

Why:
- It appears to offer a good balance between public urban-green-blue context and established community stewardship.
- The official BBMP park portal's lack of bird-inspection data suggests a possible administrative data gap worth checking, but it is not evidence that no other surveys exist.
- A discussion with MAPSAS and the current site authority can quickly determine whether the pilot would be complementary or duplicative.
- It avoids beginning with a protected conservation reserve where the legal and ecological safeguards are substantially more demanding.

**Important qualification:** This recommendation is about which site to investigate first. It is not a conclusion that Kaikondrahalli has the greatest biodiversity, the poorest monitoring, the greatest conservation need, or that MAPSAS / the authorities have agreed to collaborate.

If MAPSAS confirms that existing work already covers the proposed indicators, STF should not force the project there. Jakkur should be considered only if JaLaPoshan identifies a specific unmet need. If neither site presents a genuine gap, the correct decision is to defer the pilot and preserve funds for a better-justified opportunity.
