## 1. Purpose and executive assessment

This review is the next recommended step before STF adopts the 12-month roadmap as an executable plan. Its purpose is to establish what STF is legally and operationally authorised, funded and equipped to do; what evidence is still needed; and which commitments must remain on hold.

### 1.1 Current assessment

**Overall organisational readiness: NOT ASSESSED — critical evidence not yet supplied or verified.**

This is not a negative judgement about STF. The repository contains strategic and technical proposals, but the materials reviewed do not establish STF's legal form, registration status, governing documents, current board/trustee/committee composition, tax registrations, FCRA status, bank mandates, accounts, insurance, staff capacity, cash balance or delegated authority.

Do not infer these facts from the organisation's name, website, repository ownership, founder role, public presence or project documentation.

### 1.2 Immediate recommendation

Until the evidence register in this document is completed and reviewed by the authorised governing body:

- Do not represent the 12-month roadmap budget as approved or funded.
- Do not sign new material contracts, accept restricted grants, employ staff or commit to a field programme without verified authority, funds and controls.
- Do not accept foreign contribution unless qualified counsel confirms the organisation's eligibility and all applicable FCRA requirements are satisfied.
- Do not claim CSR implementing-agency eligibility without checking the entity type, tax registrations, track record where relevant and CSR-1/CRN status.
- Do not collect biological resources, associated traditional knowledge or personal data for a new programme until applicable permissions, rights, consent and data controls are assessed.
- Do not describe Botanica Indica as scientifically validated or production-ready on the basis of its documentation alone.

## 3. Readiness rating method

Use the following status for every item in Sections 4–11:

- **Green — verified:** Primary evidence checked, current, complete and approved by the appropriate authority.
- **Amber — partial:** Evidence exists but is incomplete, conditional, nearing expiry or awaiting formal approval.
- **Red — gap/blocker:** Required evidence is absent, invalid, overdue, materially inconsistent or an applicable requirement is unmet.
- **Not assessed:** No adequate evidence has been examined. This is the default status for this review.

Every rating must include: evidence reference; responsible owner; date checked; expiry/review date; decision required; and residual risk. Do not convert “not assessed” into green by assumption.

## 4. Legal identity and organisational form

### 4.1 Questions to establish

1. What is STF's exact legal name as registered?
2. Is STF a Section 8 company, registered society, public charitable trust, another legal entity, or an unincorporated initiative?
3. Which statute and state/central authority govern its registration?
4. What is the registration number, date, registered office and current status?
5. What are its objects and powers? Do they cover biodiversity conservation, education, research, technology development, public awareness, grants, employment, partnerships and the proposed activities?
6. Are there restrictions on commercial activity, income generation, asset ownership, intellectual property, use of surplus, or transfer of assets?
7. Are all changes in office bearers, directors, trustees, registered office or constitutional documents filed as required?
8. Does the entity have capacity to own or license software, datasets, trademarks, equipment and other intellectual property?

### 4.2 Evidence checklist

- Registration certificate and current official registry extract.
- Current constitution: memorandum and articles, trust deed, society memorandum/rules/bye-laws, or equivalent.
- Amendments and filing acknowledgements.
- Registered-office evidence and any required local registrations.
- Current register of directors/trustees/members and appointment/resignation resolutions.
- Objects-to-activities mapping reviewed by Indian counsel.
- Any required licences, local permissions or professional registrations.

### 4.3 Entity-choice caution

Do not choose or change legal form merely because one structure appears more fundable. A Section 8 company, society and public charitable trust have different governance, filing, state-law, tax, member/trustee and administrative consequences. The appropriate form depends on STF's actual activities, control model, intended funding, asset ownership, liability allocation and long-term plans. Obtain a written comparison from qualified Indian counsel and a chartered accountant familiar with non-profits before restructuring.

**Decision gate L1:** Legal identity and authority to undertake the proposed activities are documented and confirmed.

## 5. Governance, accountability and decision rights

### 5.1 Minimum governance architecture

The governing body should approve and periodically review:

- Mission, objects and strategic priorities.
- Annual plan, budget and material budget revisions.
- Appointment, evaluation and removal of the executive lead.
- Delegation of authority and signing limits.
- Financial controls, procurement and related-party transactions.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations and recusals.
- Risk appetite and risk register.
- Whistleblowing, complaints and grievance handling.
- Data, privacy, scientific integrity, community knowledge and safeguarding policies.
- Partnership, fundraising and public-claims approval.
- Asset, intellectual property and records management.
- Continuity, succession and closure arrangements.

### 5.2 Separation of duties

Where staffing permits, separate these responsibilities:

- Requesting expenditure.
- Approving expenditure.
- Making payment.
- Recording transactions.
- Reconciling bank statements.
- Certifying deliverables.
- Independently reviewing performance and impact.

If a small team cannot fully segregate duties, the governing body should document compensating controls, such as a second signatory, independent monthly review and periodic external accounting review.

### 5.3 Evidence checklist

- Current governing-body roster and appointment terms.
- Meeting calendar, quorum rules, minutes and resolutions.
- Delegation-of-authority matrix.
- Conflict-of-interest policy and annual declarations.
- Related-party transaction register.
- Code of conduct and whistleblowing/grievance procedure.
- Annual strategy, plan and approved budget.
- Decision and risk registers.
- Succession and continuity plan.

**Decision gate G1:** Every material programme, contract, grant and expenditure has an identifiable approving authority.

## 6. Tax, accounting and statutory compliance

### 6.1 Tax-law transition — important 2026 verification

The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into effect from 1 April 2026. The Income Tax Department's current guidance states that fresh applications made on or after that date for provisional registration of a qualifying new non-profit use **Form 104**, corresponding to former Form 10A. Earlier registrations and pending applications are governed by the applicable transition provisions. Do not blindly reuse old Form 10A/10AB checklists or old section references without a tax professional checking the current Act and Rules.

Primary starting points:
- Income Tax Department, Form 104 and 106 guidance: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/newformpage/forms/form104%26106
- Income Tax Department, forms and transition FAQs: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/income-tax-forms

### 6.2 Questions and evidence

- Does STF have a PAN and TAN, where applicable?
- What is the current tax registration/approval status and under which current provision?
- Does it have donor deduction approval, and is it current?
- Are all required income-tax returns, audit reports, donation statements and other forms filed?
- Are donations receipted and reconciled to bank records?
- Are restricted grants separately tracked by donor, project, purpose, period and permitted use?
- Are TDS, payroll, GST and other tax obligations assessed for actual activities?
- Are books maintained on a consistent accounting basis by a competent accountant?
- Are annual financial statements and audits completed where required?
- Are fixed assets, software, equipment, grants receivable and liabilities recorded?
- Are foreign exchange, grants and cross-border payments subject to a documented review?

### 6.3 Minimum financial controls

- Approved annual budget and monthly actual-versus-budget reporting.
- Bank reconciliation at least monthly.
- Two-person approval for payments above a governing-body-set threshold.
- No personal-account collection or commingling of organisational funds.
- Written procurement and expense-reimbursement rules.
- Supporting invoices, contracts, approvals and proof of delivery.
- Restricted-fund ledger and donor reporting calendar.
- Asset register and periodic physical verification.
- Independent audit/review appropriate to legal obligations and scale.

**Decision gate F1:** A chartered accountant confirms current filings, tax status, accounting controls and financial reporting obligations in writing.

## 7. Foreign contribution and international funding

### 7.1 Current legal position must be checked carefully

The FCRA framework is not a general ban on foreign donations. Eligible entities may receive foreign contribution only through the legally permitted registration or prior-permission route and must comply with the applicable receipt, utilisation, banking and reporting conditions.

A significant current-law distinction: the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 was introduced in Lok Sabha on 25 March 2026 and, according to the latest parliamentary tracking located for this review, was referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee and remained pending. It must not be described as enacted law unless a later Gazette notification confirms enactment and commencement. Separately, the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026 were notified on 22 June 2026. Obtain current counsel advice on the Rules and any later legislative developments before applying or accepting funds.

Sources:
- PRS India, FCRA Amendment Bill 2026 status and analysis: https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-foreign-contribution-regulation-amendment-bill-2026
- PRS India, Joint Committee activity (latest located hearing 12 November 2026): https://prsindia.org/parliamentary-committees/joint-committee-on-the-foreign-contribution-regulation-amendment-bill-2026
- MHA FCRA portal: https://fcraonline.nic.in/
- FCRA Amendment Rules 2026 text: https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2026/FCRA_Rules_2026.pdf

### 7.2 Questions and safeguards

- Does STF intend to solicit or accept any foreign-source donation, grant, transfer, article or other contribution?
- Has counsel assessed whether any proposed receipt falls within the FCRA definition of foreign contribution?
- Does STF hold valid FCRA registration or project/source-specific prior permission, if required?
- Do the certificate, permitted purposes, geography and conditions cover the proposed activity?
- Are designated banking arrangements, accounting, utilisation, annual returns and audit controls in place?
- Have key functionaries been briefed on personal and organisational compliance exposure?
- Are foreign grants separated from domestic funds and tracked by source, purpose and project?
- Are foreign-source in-kind contributions, travel, hospitality, equipment and transfers reviewed?
- Has the organisation assessed the consequences of expiry, non-renewal, surrender or cancellation, including assets created wholly or partly from foreign contributions?

**Strict rule:** Do not receive foreign contribution into an ordinary domestic account or assume that a foreign donor's payment processor, crowdfunding platform, university partner or domestic intermediary automatically makes a receipt domestic. Obtain a written FCRA classification and approval before acceptance.

**Decision gate FC1:** No foreign contribution is solicited or accepted until written FCRA advice and all applicable permissions are verified.

## 8. CSR funding readiness

CSR funding is not a general-purpose donation route. Eligibility to act as a CSR implementing agency depends on the entity category and the applicable CSR Policy Rules. The National CSR Exchange Portal describes eligibility categories, including qualifying Section 8 companies, registered public trusts and registered societies, with tax registrations and track-record conditions applying to certain categories. A valid CSR Registration Number (CRN) is required for an implementing agency to register on that portal.

Sources:
- MCA CSR FAQ: https://www.mca.gov.in/Ministry/pdf/FAQ_CSR.pdf
- National CSR Exchange Portal FAQ: https://csrxchange.gov.in/Frontend/faq

### Readiness questions

- Does STF's legal form fall within an eligible category?
- Does it hold the required current tax registrations/approvals?
- Does the relevant eligibility route require a three-year track record of similar activities, and can this be evidenced?
- Has CSR-1 been filed and a valid CRN obtained, if applicable?
- Does the proposed programme map to Schedule VII and the donor company's CSR policy?
- Is the programme designed as a project with outputs, outcomes, budget, monitoring and reporting?
- Are related-party, conflict-of-interest, procurement and utilisation controls adequate?
- Are the roles of STF, corporate donor, delivery partner and beneficiaries clear?

Do not describe STF as “CSR eligible” or solicit CSR funds as an eligible implementing agency until a professional has confirmed the exact route and documentary status.

**Decision gate CSR1:** CSR status and project eligibility are documented before proposals make a compliance claim.

## 10. Partnerships, community rights and scientific integrity

### 10.1 Partnership due diligence

Before signing an MoU or announcing a partner, verify:

- The legal identity and authority of the proposed counterparty.
- The actual named individuals and their institutional roles.
- Scope, deliverables, costs, milestones and termination.
- Ownership and licensing of data, images, code, reports and other outputs.
- Publication, attribution, confidentiality and public-communications rights.
- Liability, insurance, safeguarding, dispute resolution and governing law.
- Permissions for field access, collection, sample transfer, research and publication.
- Data security, retention, breach response and deletion.
- Community authority, consent, benefit-sharing and grievance channels where applicable.

An MoU does not itself replace statutory permissions, community consent or a detailed data/IP agreement.

### 10.2 Biological resources and associated knowledge — India

The National Biodiversity Authority states that the Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 superseded the 2004 Rules and that the 2025 amendment came into force on 1 November 2025. The NBA also lists the 2025 Access and Benefit Sharing Regulations. Its current FAQ distinguishes research, bio-survey/bio-utilisation, commercial utilisation and IPR activities, and explains that obligations vary by the person's category and activity.

Sources:
- NBA FAQ: https://nbaindia.nic.in/about-us/faqs
- NBA notification and guidelines: https://nbaindia.nic.in/public-information/notification-guidelines
- Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended): https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/21545/1/the_biological_diversity_act%2C_2002.pdf

For STF, do not assume that every observation, photograph, public-domain record, herbarium specimen, tissue sample, sequence, traditional knowledge record or commercial AI dataset has the same legal status. Classify the activity and material, source, person, purpose, geography, transfer, benefit and intended downstream use. Obtain a qualified biodiversity-law assessment before access, transfer, commercialisation, IPR filing or use of associated traditional knowledge. Also check wildlife, forest, protected-area, research institution and state-specific permissions as relevant.

### 10.3 Community knowledge and Indigenous/local rights

- Do not scrape, publish or train AI on community-held or restricted knowledge merely because it is visible or shared in a meeting.
- Establish who has authority to consent and under which community processes.
- Agree permitted purpose, access, attribution, confidentiality, retention, benefit-sharing and withdrawal/complaint procedures.
- Respect restrictions on sacred, seasonal, gender-specific, location-sensitive or otherwise confidential knowledge.
- Ensure community representatives can review interpretations and request correction or removal where agreed and lawful.
- Do not treat a single individual as automatically authorised to disclose collective knowledge.
- Use the CARE Principles as a governance reference alongside FAIR, not as a substitute for local protocols or law: https://www.gida-global.org/care

**Decision gate C1:** No collection, ingestion, public release or AI use of community knowledge until rights, authority, consent, purpose and benefit arrangements are documented.

## 11. Data protection, cybersecurity and sensitive biodiversity information

### 11.1 DPDP transition verified for November 2026

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 have a phased commencement schedule. The Gazette notification dated 13 November 2025 brought some provisions into force immediately, some after one year, and the principal processing obligations in Sections 3–17 and associated provisions after 18 months (13 May 2027). As of this review date, STF should prepare now rather than assume the entire Act is already in force or that the deferred provisions can be ignored.

Sources:
- MeitY DPDP Rules 2025 and notifications: https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa
- Gazette commencement notification, 13 November 2025: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf

### 11.2 Readiness questions

- What personal data is collected from users, contributors, experts, staff, partners and children?
- What are the purpose, lawful basis/notice, retention period and deletion process for each category?
- Who is the data fiduciary and who are processors/vendors?
- Are contracts, notices, consent flows, access requests and grievance handling being prepared for the applicable commencement dates?
- Are data transfers, cloud regions, vendor access, logs, analytics and backups mapped?
- Are breach detection, response, escalation and notification procedures documented?
- Are access controls, MFA, secrets management, encryption, backups, vulnerability management and incident response operational?
- Are children likely to use the app or educational activities, and if so, what age-assurance, parental-consent and safety design is needed under applicable law?
- Are precise locations, rare-species records, fieldworker locations and community knowledge treated as potentially sensitive even where they are not personal data?

### 11.3 Sensitive biodiversity information

Personal-data law is not the only reason to protect location data. Public disclosure of threatened species, nests, roosts, rare plant localities, culturally sensitive sites or collection points can create ecological or community harm. Apply access tiers, generalisation, suppression, delayed release, audit logs and documented release authority. The data owner and scientific risk reviewer should approve public release.

**Decision gate D1:** A documented data inventory, privacy/security risk assessment, retention plan and sensitive-location release policy exist before public collection or release.

## 12. Communications, claims and reputation

Every public statement should distinguish:

- **Mission/aspiration:** what STF intends to achieve.
- **Activity:** what STF has actually done.
- **Output:** what it has produced.
- **Outcome:** what changed for users, partners or institutions.
- **Impact:** evidenced longer-term ecological or social change, with credible attribution/contribution analysis.

Before publishing a claim, record source, date, method, geographic scope, denominator, uncertainty, limitations and approval owner.

Do not claim:
- “India's leading” or “world's first” without a defined and independently evidenced comparison.
- Expert validation without named, qualified reviewers and recorded scope.
- Species identification accuracy without representative testing and disclosed limitations.
- Conservation impact from app downloads, observations, page views or event attendance alone.
- Community partnership, endorsement or consent without documented authorisation.
- “CSR eligible”, “FCRA approved”, “tax exempt” or “80G approved” without current evidence.

Maintain a correction policy and public contact for concerns.

## 13. Programme and pilot readiness

No field or community pilot should begin until the programme has:

1. A defined problem, location, participants and intended beneficiaries.
2. A theory of change that identifies assumptions and external factors.
3. A partner and community engagement plan with authority and consent.
4. Required permits, ethics review, land access and safety approvals.
5. A baseline and monitoring protocol designed before intervention.
6. A budget covering delivery, maintenance, monitoring and closure.
7. A named accountable owner and independent reviewer.
8. Data, media, sample, knowledge and publication rights agreements.
9. A safeguarding, incident and grievance process.
10. A post-pilot maintenance and exit plan.

A pilot may appropriately be deferred. A no-go decision is a valid result where evidence, capacity, permissions or funding are inadequate.

## 15. First 30-day review process

### Week 1 — Establish the facts

- Appoint one accountable coordinator for the readiness review.
- Assemble the current registration certificate, constitution, governing-body records and statutory filing acknowledgements.
- Confirm the exact legal name, entity type, registered office and authorised signatories.
- Obtain current bank, finance, funding and liability summaries through a secure channel.
- Identify what information must remain confidential and where it will be stored.

### Week 2 — Professional verification

- Engage an Indian non-profit lawyer to review legal form, objects, governance and contracts.
- Engage a chartered accountant experienced with non-profits to review tax status, accounts, audits and reporting.
- Obtain specialist FCRA advice before any foreign funding activity.
- Obtain biodiversity-law advice for planned access to biological resources, associated knowledge, samples, sequence data or commercial uses.
- Identify privacy/security and safeguarding advice proportionate to actual planned activity.

### Week 3 — Governance and capacity

- Hold a governing-body meeting to confirm priorities, decision rights, risk appetite and expenditure limits.
- Assign accountable owners and realistic time commitments.
- Approve conflict-of-interest, finance, procurement, safeguarding, data and public-claims policies or a dated plan to complete them.
- Review all proposed partnerships and commitments; suspend any that lack authority or required permissions.

### Week 4 — Readiness decision

- Review OR-01 to OR-22 with evidence and owners.
- Classify each as Green, Amber, Red or Not assessed.
- Approve a realistic cash-based budget and runway.
- Decide which workstreams may proceed, which require conditions, and which must pause.
- Publish only a non-confidential summary of organisational readiness and commitments.

## 16. Leadership decisions required

The authorised governing body should record explicit decisions on:

1. Confirmation of STF's legal identity and governing authority.
2. Appointment of an accountable executive/programme lead and readiness-review coordinator.
3. Appointment of finance and statutory-compliance owners.
4. Approval of expenditure thresholds, bank controls and procurement rules.
5. Whether STF intends to pursue domestic individual donations, domestic grants, CSR, foreign contribution or a combination; each route requires separate eligibility review.
6. Whether to fund independent scientific review of Botanica Indica as the immediate programme priority.
7. Whether field pilots and community knowledge collection remain on hold until all readiness gates are passed.
8. Whether the 12-month roadmap is adopted, amended or deferred.
9. What budget is actually available in cash, what is restricted and what is only anticipated.
10. Which public statements STF is authorised to make now.

## 17. Recommended decision thresholds

- **Proceed with desk research and internal documentation:** May proceed under existing authority and normal confidentiality controls.
- **Proceed with ordinary domestic expenditure:** Only within verified legal authority, approved budget and financial controls.
- **Enter a material contract or hire:** Only after authority, budget, scope, IP, liability and employment/consultancy review.
- **Accept CSR funding:** Only after route-specific eligibility and project compliance are confirmed.
- **Accept foreign contribution:** Only after written FCRA advice and all applicable approvals/registration are in force.
- **Collect personal data or run user beta:** Only after privacy, security, notice/consent and safeguarding controls appropriate to the activity are approved.
- **Access biological resources or associated knowledge:** Only after activity-specific biodiversity-law review, permissions and benefit-sharing/community safeguards as applicable.
- **Launch a conservation pilot:** Only after permissions, partner/community arrangements, baseline, safety, budget, maintenance and evaluation are approved.
- **Claim ecological impact:** Only with appropriate evidence and transparent limitations.
