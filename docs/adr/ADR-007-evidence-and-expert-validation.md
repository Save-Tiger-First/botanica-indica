# ADR-007: Evidence and Expert Validation

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-09-22

## Context

Botanical identification cannot be treated as a single prediction produced by an AI model.

A reliable botanical intelligence system must be able to distinguish between:

- An AI-generated candidate
- Supporting evidence
- A user-provided identification
- Community confirmation
- Expert assessment
- Scientific verification

The system must also preserve disagreements, uncertainty, source provenance, and the reasoning or evidence associated with an identification.

Evidence and expert validation therefore require explicit architectural support rather than being treated as metadata attached to an identification result.

## Decision

Botanica Indica will implement **evidence** and **expert validation** as distinct but interconnected capabilities.

Evidence will provide the factual and scientific basis associated with an observation or identification.

Expert validation will provide a human scientific assessment of an observation, identification, or evidence set.

Neither evidence aggregation nor expert validation will silently overwrite the underlying observation or identification history.

## 1. Evidence as a First-Class Concept

Evidence will be represented as a first-class domain concept.

Evidence may include:

- Taxonomic records
- Flora treatments
- Herbarium records
- Scientific publications
- Biodiversity databases
- Verified observations
- Distribution records
- Morphological characteristics
- Geographic compatibility
- Expert observations
- User-provided information
- AI-generated candidate predictions
- Images and other media

Evidence must retain its provenance.

An evidence item should therefore be traceable to its original source whenever technically and legally possible.

## 2. Evidence Provenance

Evidence records should preserve information such as:

- Source identity
- Source type
- Source version
- Original identifier
- Source URL or reference
- Retrieval date
- Publication or update date where available
- Licensing information
- Relevant taxonomic context
- Transformation or processing history
- Relationship to the observation or identification

Evidence derived from another source must not be represented as original project data.

The provenance principles defined in **ADR-004: Data and Scientific Provenance** apply to all evidence.

## 3. Evidence Does Not Automatically Establish Truth

The existence of evidence does not automatically establish that an identification is correct.

Evidence may be:

- Strong
- Weak
- Incomplete
- Conflicting
- Outdated
- Context-dependent
- Based on a different taxonomic treatment
- Generated by an automated system

The platform should preserve these distinctions rather than reducing all evidence to a single undifferentiated confidence value.

## 4. Multiple and Conflicting Evidence

The system must support multiple evidence items for the same observation or identification.

Conflicting evidence must be retained rather than silently discarded.

For example:

```text
Observation
    │
    ├── Evidence A → supports Taxon A
    ├── Evidence B → supports Taxon A
    ├── Evidence C → supports Taxon B
    └── Expert Assessment → unresolved

The platform should make such conflicts visible where appropriate.

Resolution of conflicting evidence must be traceable.

5. Evidence Relationships

Evidence should be capable of expressing relationships such as:

Supports
Contradicts
Provides context for
Derived from
Supersedes
Refers to
Validates
Requires review

The exact production data model will be defined during domain and API design.

6. Expert Validation

Expert validation will be treated as a distinct workflow.

An expert may review:

Observation media
Candidate identifications
Taxonomic relationships
Morphological evidence
Geographic evidence
Existing scientific sources
Previous community identifications
Conflicting evidence

The expert's assessment should be recorded separately from the original AI or user-generated identification.

7. Expert Identity and Qualifications

Where expert information is made public, the platform may expose appropriate professional or institutional information according to the person's permissions and the platform's policies.

The system should support recording relevant validation context such as:

Reviewer identity
Institutional affiliation where applicable
Review date
Scope of review
Taxonomic expertise where voluntarily provided
Review outcome
Reviewer comments
Supporting evidence

The platform must not imply qualifications that have not been established.

8. Validation States

The system will support explicit validation states.

The initial conceptual states are:

Submitted
    ↓
AI Identified
    ↓
User Confirmed
    ↓
Expert Reviewed
    ↓
Scientifically Verified

These states represent different levels or forms of validation and must not be interpreted as a simple universal ranking of correctness.

An observation may move between states as new evidence becomes available.

The system must preserve the history of these transitions.

9. Expert Review Does Not Rewrite History

When an expert disagrees with an AI prediction or previous identification, the system must preserve the previous result.

For example:

AI prediction:
Taxon A

Expert assessment:
Taxon B

Historical record:
AI predicted Taxon A
Expert assessed Taxon B

The system may expose the current validated interpretation while retaining the historical identification and its provenance.

This supports reproducibility, auditing, model evaluation, and scientific transparency.

10. Reviewer Assessment

Expert validation should support more than a binary correct/incorrect result.

Possible outcomes include:

Confirmed
Rejected
Alternative identification proposed
Identification uncertain
Additional evidence required
Taxonomic issue identified
Geographic inconsistency identified
Media insufficient for determination

The final domain model should allow future validation outcomes without requiring architectural redesign.

11. Comments and Scientific Reasoning

Where appropriate, reviewers should be able to provide explanatory comments.

Comments may describe:

Diagnostic morphological characters
Differences between similar species
Relevant geographic information
Taxonomic interpretation
Supporting literature
Reasons for rejecting a candidate
Additional evidence required

Scientific reasoning should remain associated with the relevant review or evidence rather than being stored as an unexplained global annotation.

12. Community Contributions

The platform may support community contributions to observations and identifications.

Community contributions should remain distinguishable from expert validation.

Community participation may include:

Suggested identification
Confirmation
Correction
Additional photograph
Habitat information
Distribution information
Discussion
Evidence reference

Community agreement alone must not automatically be represented as scientific verification.

13. Evidence and Identification Relationship

Identification results and evidence should be linked explicitly.

Conceptually:

Observation
    │
    ├── Identification
    │       ├── Candidate Taxon
    │       ├── Model
    │       └── Confidence
    │
    ├── Evidence
    │       ├── Taxonomic Source
    │       ├── Distribution Source
    │       └── Morphological Evidence
    │
    └── Validation
            ├── User
            ├── Community
            └── Expert

This separation allows the system to explain not only what was identified, but also why that identification was proposed and who or what subsequently reviewed it.

14. Evidence Quality and Completeness

Where practical, the platform should communicate the quality and completeness of available evidence.

Examples include:

Source quality
Evidence completeness
Number of independent supporting sources
Presence of conflicting evidence
Recency of evidence
Geographic relevance
Taxonomic relevance
Expert review status

These indicators must be descriptive and traceable to underlying data.

They should not be converted into an unexplained universal score.

15. Scientific Verification

"Scientifically Verified" represents a specific validation state and must not be assumed merely because:

An AI model has high confidence
Many users agree
A species is common
An external provider returns the same result
A taxonomic database contains the name

Scientific verification must have an identifiable basis and an auditable provenance trail.

The exact criteria for this state will be defined through future scientific governance and validation workflow decisions.

16. Taxonomic Conflicts

Evidence and expert validation may expose disagreements between taxonomic sources.

Such disagreements must be handled through the taxonomy reconciliation architecture defined in ADR-005: Taxonomy Reconciliation.

An expert review must not silently modify the platform's taxonomic backbone.

Taxonomic changes and identification assessments remain separate operations.

17. Evidence Versioning

Evidence may change over time.

Sources may:

Publish new editions
Correct records
Change taxonomic treatments
Update distributions
Remove or replace records
Change URLs or APIs

The platform should preserve sufficient source and version information to understand which evidence supported an identification at a particular point in time.

Historical evidence should not be silently replaced by newer evidence when doing so would destroy reproducibility.

18. Audit History

Important evidence and validation actions should be auditable.

The system should retain appropriate history for:

Evidence creation
Evidence updates
Evidence removal
Identification changes
Expert reviews
Validation-state transitions
Corrections
Disagreements
Taxonomic changes affecting an identification

Audit requirements must respect privacy, security, and applicable data-retention policies.

19. API and Platform Integration

Evidence and expert validation functionality will be exposed through stable platform APIs.

API contracts should support:

Evidence retrieval
Evidence provenance
Identification evidence relationships
Validation history
Expert review workflows
Community contributions
Conflicting evidence
Validation status
Audit information

Client applications must not implement independent versions of the scientific validation workflow.

The platform remains the authoritative system for these domain operations.

20. Privacy and Access Control

Not all evidence or reviewer information must necessarily be public.

The platform should support appropriate access controls for:

Private observations
Sensitive locations
Reviewer information
Institutional information
Internal review discussions
Pre-publication scientific material
Sensitive conservation evidence

Public presentation of evidence must follow applicable licensing and privacy requirements.

21. Architectural Consequences

This decision provides:

Explicit evidence provenance
Transparent identification reasoning
Traceable expert validation
Preservation of disagreements
Historical auditability
Separation between AI, community, and expert conclusions
Better scientific reproducibility
A foundation for future research and institutional workflows

It also introduces additional complexity in:

Evidence modelling
Provenance management
Review workflows
Access control
Audit history
Source versioning
Conflict representation
Scientific governance

This complexity is intentional because evidence and expert validation are fundamental scientific capabilities of the ecosystem.
